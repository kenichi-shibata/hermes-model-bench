Short answer: **it runs, but it's not Elo.** It's a flat ±32 points per game, regardless of the players' ratings.

What I found in `elo_engine.py`:

```python
def update(winner, loser, ratings):
    ratings[winner] = ratings.get(winner, 1500) + 32
    ratings[loser] = ratings.get(loser, 1500) - 32
```

The problem — real Elo is:

- Expected score: `E = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_loser − R_winner) / 400))`
- Update: `R_new = R_old + K × (score − E)`

This engine skips the expected-score calculation entirely, so the current ratings are never actually used. Consequences:

1. **Upsets aren't rewarded** — beating a 2500-rated opponent gives the same +32 as beating a 1000-rated one.
2. **Favorites aren't penalized less** — a top player farm­ing wins against beginners keeps gaining full points forever, so ratings never stabilize.
3. K=32 is applied as a fixed amount instead of scaling with expectation.
