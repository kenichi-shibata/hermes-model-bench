## Verdict: yes, the alert is broken

Cross-referencing the two files shows a false-negative during a live outage:

| Time (UTC) | service_logs.txt | upstream_status_mock.json |
|---|---|---|
| 09:55:00 | — | payments-api **major_outage**, no ETA |
| 10:00:01 | INFO healthy | still major_outage |
| 10:01:01 | INFO healthy | still major_outage |
| 10:02:01 | INFO healthy | still major_outage |
| 10:03:01 | WARN upstream 503 | still major_outage |
| 10:04:01 | INFO healthy (own health check green) | still major_outage |

Two failures, one root cause:

1. **Blind window (09:55–10:03):** the outage was active ~8 minutes before any log line showed it. Three consecutive "healthy" lines (10:00–10:02) while payments-api was already down.
2. **False recovery (10:04):** one minute after the 503 WARN, the service logs "healthy (own health check green)" — but the dependency is still in `major_outage` with no ETA. The health check measures **only its own liveness**, so a single transient 503 gets logged once and forgotten. There's no sustained alert state for an ongoing upstream outage.

The alert conflates "my process is up" with "the system works," with no hysteresis to keep alerting while the dependency stays down.

## Recommendation (guidance only — no code exists here to edit)

The health check should ingest dependency status (the `upstream_status_mock.json`-equivalent) and only revert to green once the upstream reports **resolved/healthy** — not merely because a recent error log line is absent. That alone closes both the blind window and the false-recovery.
