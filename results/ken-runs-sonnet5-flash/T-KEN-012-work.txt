## Finding: Service X is fine — the host is the bottleneck

**Service X's own logs** (`service_x_logs.txt:1-2`): only 2 requests, handled in **45ms and 52ms**, no errors, no timeout. No evidence of an internal defect.

## What's happening
An unrelated batch job is saturating CPU/memory on the shared host and driving swap to its limit. Service X's internal request timers stay fast, but its responses are delayed *outside* the process — scheduling starvation, page faults, swap I/O. Classic **host resource contention**, not an app bug.

## Recommended action (ops, not code)
1. **Do not touch Service X code** — nothing in its logs supports a fix there.
2. **Relocate or throttle `unrelated_batch_job.py`**: move to a separate host / off-peak window, or bound it with cgroup/K8s `resources.limits` so it can't starve co-located services.
3. **Add swap headroom** and alert at ~80% swap used so this is caught before it hits users.
4. **Monitor per-process CPU/mem at the host level** — Service X's own metrics look healthy despite the real problem, so app-level logs alone won't catch this class of issue.
